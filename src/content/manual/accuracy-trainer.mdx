The Accuracy Trainer listens to an electronic drum kit over MIDI and measures every hit: how early (rushing) or late (dragging) it was, in milliseconds. You can watch it live, hide the numbers and get a summary every few bars, or turn the click off and see how well you hold the tempo on your own.

<Experimental feature="Accuracy Trainer" />

<Pro /> The Accuracy Trainer needs Makonome Pro. Nothing in it is free.

## Open the Accuracy Trainer

1. Turn it on: **Settings → Experimental Features → Accuracy Trainer**.
2. On the metronome screen, tap **••• → Accuracy Trainer**.

<Platform only="ios">
On iPhone the trainer opens as a sheet. Tap **Done** to close it. On iPad it fills the screen.
</Platform>

<Platform only="mac">
On Mac the trainer opens in its own window, next to the metronome window, so you can use both at once. You can make the window bigger or smaller. When it is wide, it shows the controls on the left and your timing on the right. If the window is already open, **••• → Accuracy Trainer** brings it to the front.
</Platform>

## Connect your drum kit

The trainer works with any drum kit or trigger module that sends MIDI notes, for example a **Yamaha EAD10**.

- **USB:** connect the kit with a USB cable. Kits that work without a driver ("class compliant") work at once. On an iPhone or iPad with a Lightning port you need a USB adapter (Apple's Lightning to USB Camera Adapter, for example).
- **Bluetooth MIDI:** pair the device with your iPhone, iPad or Mac first. Makonome has no pairing screen of its own. On Mac, pair it in **Audio MIDI Setup → Window → Show MIDI Studio → Bluetooth**.
- **Network MIDI:** a network MIDI session from a Mac also works.

The top row of the trainer shows the connected device, for example **MIDI Drum Kit**, or **No MIDI device connected**. You can plug a device in or out while the trainer is open. If a device doesn't show, tap the round arrow on the right to scan again.

<Screenshot name="trainer-no-device" alt="The Accuracy Trainer with No MIDI device connected at the top, Mode Click, the grid 1/8, Tightness ±10 ms, Live selected, All drums, Record and Sessions, the hint Start the metronome to build the beat grid, and an empty rush and drag needle" caption="The trainer before a kit is connected." />

The trainer reads the drums by their General MIDI note numbers: **Kick**, **Snare**, **Hi-hat**, **Hi-hat (open)**, **Tom (high)**, **Tom (low)**, **Crash** and **Ride**. Other notes show as "Note 52" and so on.

## Click mode: your timing against the metronome

In **Click** mode, the trainer compares each hit with the metronome's beats.

1. Pick **Click** at the top.
2. Pick the **grid**: **1/4**, **1/8**, **1/16** or **Triplet**. Each hit is measured against the nearest note of this grid. Pick the smallest note you play, for example 1/8 for eighth-note hi-hats. When the metronome's subdivision is quintuplets, sextuplets or septuplets, the grid also offers **Quint**, **Sext** or **Sept**.
3. Start the metronome. Until it plays, the trainer shows **Start the metronome to build the beat grid**.
4. Play.

<Screenshot name="trainer-live" alt="The Accuracy Trainer in Click mode with MIDI Drum Kit connected, Live selected, the needle at +0 ms for Hi-hat with velocity 53, and a list of Kick, Hi-hat and Snare with their last offset and averages" caption="Live feedback in Click mode." />

### The needle

With **Live** feedback, the needle shows your last hit. Left is **◀ RUSH** (early), right is **DRAG ▶** (late). Under it you see the offset, for example **+11 ms**, the drum and its velocity (how hard you hit, 1 to 127).

### Tightness

**Tightness** sets how strict the trainer is, from ±5 to ±30 ms (±15 at first). It sets four colour bands:

| Colour | Offset (with ±10 ms) |
|---|---|
| Green | under 10 ms |
| Yellow | under 20 ms (2 × tightness) |
| Orange | under 30 ms (3 × tightness) |
| Red | 30 ms or more |

The needle and the charts span 4 × tightness each way (±40 ms with ±10 ms). Start loose, and make it tighter as you get better.

### The drum list

Under the needle, each drum has a row with its last offset and its running numbers, for example **avg +3 ±7 · vel 70 ±7 · 42**: the average offset, how much your hits spread around it, the average velocity, and the number of hits.

On iPad and Mac (when the trainer window is wide), each drum also shows two charts:

- The **histogram** shows how your hits spread, from rush on the left, through 0, to drag on the right. A narrow peak at 0 is what you want.
- The **beat map** has one square for each note of the grid in the bar (for example 1, &, 2, &… with 1/8). Each square takes the colour of your average timing on that note, so you see where in the bar you rush or drag. Empty squares had no hits yet.

### Focus on one drum

To look at one drum only, tap it in the list, or pick it in the **All drums** menu. The other drums dim, and the needle shows only that drum, for example **Snare only**. Tap the drum again, or pick **All drums**, to see everything.

## Practice mode: hidden numbers

Seeing your timing on every hit can make you depend on it. In **Practice** mode the trainer hides the numbers while you play and shows a summary from time to time instead.

1. Pick **Practice** (next to **Live**).
2. Pick **Reveal every**: **4 bars** or **8 bars**.
3. Play. The trainer shows **practice mode · numbers hidden**.

After every 4 or 8 bars, a box **Last 4 bars** shows each drum's average offset, its spread and the number of hits, for example **Kick +1 ms ±8 · 8 hits**. Tap **Show now** to see the summary at once. Tap the box to close it.

<Screenshot name="trainer-practice-mode" alt="The Accuracy Trainer in Practice mode with Reveal every 4 bars and Show now, and a summary box Last 4 bars: Kick +1 ms ±8, 8 hits, Hi-hat +3 ms ±8, 32 hits, Snare +2 ms ±5, 8 hits" caption="A summary after four bars." />

Practice mode works in Click mode and in Free Time.

## Free Time

**Free Time** checks if you can hold a tempo without the click. The metronome counts you in, then goes silent. You keep playing, and the trainer follows your tempo and shows how far you drift.

1. Pick **Free Time** at the top.
2. Set the tempo on the metronome's dial. The trainer shows it as **Target**, "from metronome".
3. Pick the **Count-in**: **2 bars** or **4 bars**.
4. Set **Drift ±** (1 to 6 BPM, 2 at first): how far from the target still counts as good.
5. Tap **Start**.

<Screenshot name="trainer-free-time-idle" alt="Free Time before a run: Target 120 BPM from metronome, Count-in 2 bars, Drift ±4.0 BPM, a Start button with Count-in: 2 bars of click, then silence, and 120 BPM waiting for the first bar" caption="Free Time, ready to start." />

The metronome plays the count-in bars (**Count-in · 1 bar left**), then stops clicking. The trainer shows **Silent · listening**. Keep playing.

After each bar, the big number shows your tempo, for example **124.9**, with the drift under it, for example **+4.9 BPM · 4.1 % fast**. Its colour shows how far you drifted: green under Drift ±, yellow under 2 × Drift ±, orange under 3 × Drift ±, and red beyond. Under it, a line shows your tempo bar by bar.

<Screenshot name="trainer-free-time" alt="A Free Time run in the silent phase: 124.9 in yellow, +4.9 BPM · 4.1 % fast, and a tempo line that rises slowly from bar 3 to bar 20, with Silent · listening under the Stop button" caption="Drifting faster in the silence." />

Tap **Stop** to end the run.

Good to know:

- The first bar after the silence starts only sets where your beats are. It is never read as a tempo change, so a laid-back feel is not counted as slowing down.
- Your hits are measured against your own tempo, not the click you stopped hearing. The needle and the drum list show how even you play.
- Kick and snare tell the trainer where the beat is. If you stop and start again off the beat, it finds your beat again after about two bars with kick or snare. Off-beat hi-hats don't pull it onto the "and".
- The metronome's own [count-in and auto stop](/manual/count-in-and-auto-stop/) are off during Free Time. The [Tempo Changer](/manual/trainers/#tempo-changer) pauses, and haptic feedback is off in the silence.
- A [playing collection](/manual/presets-and-collections/#play-a-collection) can't switch presets during a run.

## Record and share sessions

To keep a session:

1. Tap **Record**. The row shows the time and the hits so far, for example **00:08 · 45 hits**.
2. Play.
3. Tap **Stop**. The row shows **Saved · 3:12 · 840 hits**, with a share button.

<Screenshot name="trainer-recording" alt="The Accuracy Trainer recording: a red Stop button with the recording time and hit count under it, the needle with the last hit, and the drum list" caption="Recording a session." />

To see your recordings, tap **Sessions**. Each row shows the date, the length, the hits, the tempo and the grid, and a short line per drum. Tap a session to see its full summary.

<Screenshot name="trainer-sessions" alt="The Sessions list with six recordings, for example 27 Oct at 18:13, 06:30 · 2340 hits · 120 BPM · 1/8, Kick −3 ±9 · Hi-hat +4 ±7 · Snare +3 ±11" caption="Your recorded sessions." />

<Screenshot name="trainer-session-detail" alt="One session's detail with the header 27 Oct at 18:13 · 06:30 · 2340 hits · 120 BPM · 1/8, a text summary per drum, a share button and Delete session" caption="A session's summary, with share and delete." />

- **Share:** tap the share button. A session is a JSON file (a text file with all hits), so you can keep it, send it to a teacher, or open it in another app.
- **Delete:** swipe left on a session (or right-click it on Mac), or tap **Delete session** in its detail.

<Platform only="mac">
On Mac, **Share** opens a save dialog, so you pick a folder for the file.
</Platform>

## Accuracy Trainer reference

| Setting | Values |
|---|---|
| Mode | Click or Free Time |
| Grid (Click) | 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, Triplet, and Quint, Sext or Sept with those subdivisions |
| Tightness (Click) | ±5 to ±30 ms (±15 at first) |
| Count-in (Free Time) | 2 or 4 bars |
| Drift ± (Free Time) | 1 to 6 BPM, in steps of 0.5 (2 at first) |
| Feedback | Live or Practice |
| Reveal every (Practice) | 4 or 8 bars |
| Focus | All drums, or one drum |
