This page covers how the metronome sounds, and the other ways to follow the beat: vibration, a screen flash and visualizations.

## Sound Settings

Tap the speaker button (<Platform only="ios mac" inline>second from the right</Platform><Platform only="android" inline>second from the left</Platform> at the bottom of the metronome screen). The **Sound Settings** sheet opens with the volume, the sound and the pitch.

<Screenshot name="sound-settings" alt="The Sound Settings sheet on Android: volume at 79%, Pitch at 0 (original), and the sounds Click, Clave, Wood, Cowbell and Voice, with Wood selected" only="android" caption="Sound Settings on Android: volume, pitch, then the sounds." />

### Volume

Drag the slider to set the metronome volume, from 0 to 100%. This is the metronome's own volume. The volume buttons on your device change the overall volume.

<Platform only="ios mac">
At 0% the speaker button on the metronome screen turns red and shows a crossed-out speaker, so you can see at a glance why there is no sound.
</Platform>

### Sounds

Tap a sound to choose it.<Platform only="ios mac" inline> You hear a short preview.</Platform>

| Sound | |
|---|---|
| **Click** | Free |
| **Clave** | <Pro /> |
| **Woodblock** | <Pro /> |
| **Cowbell** | <Pro /> |
| **Voice** | <Pro /> Speaks the beat numbers: "one, two, three…" |

Each sound has a stronger version for accented beats. Without Pro, tapping a Pro sound opens the upgrade screen.

<Platform only="android">
On Android, Woodblock is called **Wood**.
</Platform>

### Voice count

**Voice** counts the beats out loud instead of clicking. It counts the beats of your meter in English, so in 7/8 it counts to seven.

<Platform only="android">
On Android the pitch doesn't work with Voice: picking Voice sets the pitch back to 0.
</Platform>

### Pitch

<Pro />

Drag the **Pitch** slider to make the sound higher or lower, from −12 to +12 semitones (one octave each way), in steps of one semitone. 0 is the original pitch. A higher or lower pitch can help the click stand out against your instrument.

Without Pro, the pitch slider is dimmed.<Platform only="ios mac" inline> Tapping it opens the upgrade screen.</Platform>

## Haptic feedback

<Platform only="ios mac">
<Experimental feature="Haptic Feedback" />
</Platform>

<Platform only="android">
<Experimental feature="Haptic Feedback" where="Settings → Experimental" />
</Platform>

<Pro />

With haptic feedback on, your <Platform only="ios mac" inline>iPhone or iPad</Platform><Platform only="android" inline>phone</Platform> vibrates on the beat. You can feel the beat when you can't hear it well, for example with loud drums.

To turn on haptic feedback:

<Platform only="ios mac">
1. Open **Settings → Experimental Features** and switch on **Haptic Feedback**.
2. On the metronome screen, tap **••• → Haptic Feedback**. The entry shows **(On)** or **(Off)**.
</Platform>

<Platform only="android">
1. Open **Settings → Experimental** and switch on **Haptic Feedback**.
2. On the metronome screen, tap **⋮ → Haptic Feedback**. While it is on, the entry's icon is colored and the ⋮ button is highlighted.
</Platform>

<Screenshot name="metronome-more-menu" alt="The ••• menu with Haptic Feedback (Off) and Screen Flash (Off) among the entries" only="ios" caption="Haptic Feedback and Screen Flash in the ••• menu." />

How it feels:

- One pulse on each beat, at the moment you hear the click. In compound meters like 6/8 counted in dotted beats, one pulse on each dotted beat.
- A stronger pulse on accented beats.
- There is no setting for the strength.
- It works at every tempo in the app, with any subdivision. The subdivision notes don't vibrate, only the beats.

<Platform only="android">
On Android the phone vibrates only while Makonome is on screen. It uses Android's touch feedback setting: if touch feedback is off in your phone's sound and vibration settings, the beat doesn't vibrate.
</Platform>

<Platform only="mac">
Haptic feedback is not available on Mac. The switch and the menu entry don't appear there.
</Platform>

## Screen flash

Screen flash tints the whole screen softly on each beat, so you can see the beat. It is free and off at first.

To turn it on or off, tap **••• → Screen Flash**<Platform only="android" inline> (⋮ on Android)</Platform> on the metronome screen. The entry shows **(On)** or **(Off)**.

How it works:

- The flash comes at the moment you hear the click, not before it.
- Accented beats flash stronger.
- It flashes on the count-in and on bars that Gap Trainer mutes, too.
- It flashes at most 3 times a second. Up to 180 beats a minute, every beat flashes. Faster than that, only the first beat of each bar flashes. At very fast tempos with short bars, it doesn't flash at all.
- With <Platform only="ios mac" inline>**Reduce Motion**</Platform><Platform only="android" inline>**Remove animations**</Platform> on (in your device's accessibility settings), the flash is softer and fades more slowly.

If flashing light is a problem for you, leave screen flash off.

## Visualizations

<Platform only="android">
Visualizations work differently on Android. They are free and not experimental:

1. On the metronome screen, tap the Visualization button in the top left corner. The **Visualization** sheet opens.
2. Under **Mode**, pick **Pendulum**, **Circular**, **Count** or **Line**. Makonome remembers your choice.

Start and stop the metronome on the metronome screen. To close the sheet, swipe it down or tap outside it.

The four styles:

- **Pendulum:** an arm swings from side to side with the beat.
- **Circular:** a ring fills up over the bar, with a dot for each beat.
- **Count:** the beat number, large.
- **Line:** a dot jumps from beat to beat along a line.
</Platform>

<Screenshot name="visualization-pendulum" alt="The Visualization sheet on Android with the Pendulum: an arm with a green dot at its end, and the Mode choices Pendulum, Circular, Count and Line" only="android" caption="The Pendulum on Android." />

<Platform only="ios mac">
<Experimental feature="Visualizations" />

A visualization shows the beat in large, full-screen moving graphics. It is free. Everything you see moves with the click you hear, not ahead of it. On iPhones with a 120 Hz screen, the motion is extra smooth.

To open a visualization:

1. Open **Settings → Experimental Features** and switch on **Visualizations**.
2. On the metronome screen, tap the visualization button in the top left corner.
3. Pick a style at the top, or swipe left and right between them.

Tap anywhere to start and stop the metronome. Tap the back arrow in the top left to go back. Makonome remembers the last style you used.

The four styles:

- **Pendulum:** an arm swings like a classic metronome and fills the screen, turning on its pivot at the bottom. The click lands when the arm reaches the end of its swing, and that end lights up for a moment, brighter on beat 1. The swing is wider on a tall screen and narrower on a short one.

  <Screenshot name="visualization-pendulum" alt="The Pendulum visualization: a green arm swinging from a pivot at the bottom" only="ios" />

- **Circular:** a ring fills up over the bar, with the beat number in the middle ("3 of 4"). The number moves on to the next beat on the click.

  <Screenshot name="visualization-circular" alt="The Circular visualization in 4/4: a green ring with the number 3 and of 4 in the middle" only="ios" />

  In x/8 and x/16 meters, the ring has one segment per beat, with gaps between the [accent groups](/manual/time-signatures/#accent-groups) and the groups written under the number.

  <Screenshot name="visualization-circular-7-8" alt="The Circular visualization in 7/8: seven segments in groups of two, two and three" only="ios" />

- **Count:** the beat number, large, changing and bouncing on each click.

  <Screenshot name="visualization-count" alt="The Count visualization showing a large number 4" only="ios" />

- **Line:** a line sweeps out to the side and back, and reaches the side on each click. A thin guide stays in the center and flashes on the off-beat, when the line passes through the middle.

  <Screenshot name="visualization-line" alt="The Line visualization: a green line to the right of a thin center mark" only="ios" />

<Platform only="mac">
Visualizations work the same way on Mac. Click anywhere in the window to start and stop.
</Platform>
</Platform>
