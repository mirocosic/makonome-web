The time signature (or meter) sets how many beats a bar has and which note gets the beat. All time signatures, subdivisions and rests are free.

## Open the Time Signature sheet

Tap <Platform only="ios mac" inline>the button with the notes, second from the left at the bottom of the metronome screen</Platform><Platform only="android" inline>the button that shows the meter, for example **4/4**, right of the play button. You can also tap the meter label above the dial</Platform>. The **Time Signature** sheet opens. Every change works at once, also while the metronome plays.

<Platform only="ios">
To close the sheet, swipe it down or tap outside it. On iPhone, if not everything fits, the sheet opens smaller: drag it up to see the rest.
</Platform>

<Platform only="mac">
On Mac, close the sheet with the ✕ button in its top right corner.
</Platform>

<Platform only="android">
On Android the sheet opens at full height and scrolls if not everything fits. To close it, swipe it down, tap outside it, or use the back gesture.
</Platform>

The sheet has these sections, from top to bottom:

- **Common:** one-tap meters.
- **Custom:** any meter from 1 to 16 beats.
- **Count in:** how you count compound meters like 6/8. Only shown for those meters.
- **Accent groups:** accents for meters with five or more beats, and for compound meters.
- **Subdivision:** extra clicks between the beats, and the rests inside the beat.

The **Count in** section is about what you count. It is not the [count-in](/manual/count-in-and-auto-stop/#count-in) that plays before the music.

## Common meters

The **Common** section has eight buttons: **2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/8, 9/8** and **12/8**. Tap one to switch to it.

When you pick a meter, Makonome sets its default accents. In x/4 and x/2 meters, beat 1 is accented. In x/8 and x/16 meters, the first beat of each group is accented (see [Accent groups](#accent-groups)).

## Custom meters

To set any other meter, turn the two wheels in the **Custom** section:

- **Top number** (beats in a bar): 1 to 16
- **Bottom number** (the note that gets the beat): 2, 4, 8 or 16

For example, 5 over 8 gives 5/8, and 3 over 2 gives 3/2.

The BPM always counts the bottom note. In 7/8, the dial shows eighth notes per minute. The caption above the number tells you which note it counts.

## Accent groups

Odd meters are usually felt in groups of two and three beats. 7/8 can be 2+2+3, 2+3+2 or 3+2+2. The **Accent groups** section sets these groups in one tap.

To set the accent groups:

1. Pick a meter with five or more beats, or a compound meter like 6/8.
2. In **Accent groups**, tap a group, for example **2+2+3**.

The first beat of each group gets an accent. The dots under the buttons show the pattern. **Downbeat** accents only beat 1.

<Screenshot name="metronome-7-8" alt="The metronome playing 7/8 at 210 BPM: seven beat rings in three groups, two, two and three, with beats 1, 3 and 5 accented, and the label Eighth Notes · 7/8 · 2+2+3" caption="7/8 as 2+2+3 on the metronome screen." />

The groups also change how the metronome screen looks:

- In x/8 and x/16 meters, small gaps between the beat rings show the groups.
- The time signature label shows the groups, for example **7/8 · 2+2+3**.
- <Platform only="ios mac" inline>The Circular [visualization](/manual/sound-and-feel/#visualizations) shows the groups too.</Platform><Platform only="android" inline>The [visualizations](/manual/sound-and-feel/#visualizations) don't show the groups.</Platform>

The first button is the default for the meter. The defaults for x/8 and x/16 are:

- Groups of 3 when the beats divide by 3 (6/8 is 3+3, 9/8 is 3+3+3)
- 3+3+2 for 8 beats
- Groups of 2 ending with a 3 for other odd numbers (5/8 is 2+3, 7/8 is 2+2+3)
- Groups of 2 for other even numbers

To fine-tune, tap single beat rings on the main screen (see [Beat accents and muting](/manual/metronome-screen/#beat-accents-and-muting)). The groups and the label stay as they are.

## Compound meters and the dotted pulse

In compound meters like **6/8, 9/8** and **12/8**, you usually feel the bar in dotted beats. A bar of 6/8 has six eighth notes, but you feel two dotted quarter notes. Makonome counts these meters in dotted beats by default. This works for any meter where the top number is 6, 9, 12 or 15 and the bottom number is 8 or 16.

With a dotted beat:

- The dial shows the dotted-beat tempo. For example "♩. BPM 80", with "♪ = 240" (the eighth-note tempo) under it.
- The tempo range is 7 to 133 dotted beats a minute. That is the same 20 to 400 BPM, counted in eighth notes.
- The beat rings still show every eighth note, grouped in threes.
- Haptic feedback and screen flash follow the dotted beat.
- The subdivisions are only the ones in the first row, and there are no rests (see [Subdivisions](#subdivisions)).

<Screenshot name="metronome-6-8" alt="The metronome playing 6/8: six beat rings in two groups of three, the dial showing dotted-quarter BPM 80 with eighth note = 240 under it" only="ios" caption="6/8 counted in dotted quarters." />

To count every eighth note instead:

1. Pick the compound meter.
2. In the **Count in** section, tap **♪ Eighths** (for example "♪ Eighths · 6"). **♩. Dotted quarters** goes back to the dotted beat.

In x/16 meters the choices are **Sixteenths** and **Dotted eighths**.

**Click on the pulse only** mutes the beats between the dotted beats, so you hear only the pulse. It works only with the dotted beat.

## Subdivisions

A subdivision adds quieter clicks between the beats, so you can hear the eighth notes or triplets inside each beat.

To set a subdivision, tap one in the **Subdivision** section at the bottom of the sheet. **None** plays only the beats.

The buttons are in two rows. The first row depends on the bottom number of the meter. The second row has **Quintuplets**, **Sextuplets** and **Septuplets**: 5, 6 or 7 notes in each beat.

| Meter | First row | Second row |
|---|---|---|
| x/2 | None, Quarters, 8ths, Triplets | Quintuplets, Sextuplets, Septuplets |
| x/4 | None, 8ths, 16ths, Triplets | Quintuplets, Sextuplets, Septuplets |
| x/8 | None, 16ths, Triplets, 32nds | Quintuplets, Sextuplets, Septuplets |
| x/16 | None, 32nds, Triplets | Not offered |

**Triplets** plays three clicks for each beat.

Compound meters like 6/8 counted in dotted beats have only the first row. There the section is called **Subdivision per ♪**, because it divides each eighth note. If you count 6/8 in eighths (see [Compound meters](#compound-meters-and-the-dotted-pulse)), you get both rows, like in 7/8.

If you switch to a meter that doesn't offer your subdivision, it changes. Quintuplets, sextuplets and septuplets go back to None in an x/16 meter or a dotted beat. 16ths in 4/4 go back to None in an x/16 meter. When you switch to counting in dotted beats, the rests are cleared.

## Rests inside the beat

You can silence single notes inside the beat. For example, 16ths with the second note silent make a gallop.

Under the subdivision buttons there is a row of notes, one for each note in the beat:

- The first note is the beat, a filled disc. You can't make it a rest here.
- A played note is a solid ring.
- A rest is a dashed ring.

To make a rest, tap a note in that row. Tap it again to play it again. The rests are the same in every beat of the bar.

<Screenshot name="time-signature-rests" alt="The Time Signature sheet in 4/4 with Septuplets selected in the second row, and under it a row of seven notes: the beat as a filled disc, notes 3 and 6 as dashed rings (rests), the others as solid rings" only="ios mac" caption="Septuplets with rests on notes 3 and 6." />

Good to know:

- To silence the beat itself, mute its ring on the main screen (see [Beat accents and muting](/manual/metronome-screen/#beat-accents-and-muting)). A muted beat also silences its subdivision notes.
- When you pick another subdivision, the rests are cleared. Tapping the subdivision you already have keeps them.
- The row of notes is not there for **None**, and not for compound meters counted in dotted beats.
- The main screen shows the rests after the subdivision name, as dots: filled for a played note, empty for a rest. For example **♬ Septuplets ●●○●●○●**.
- A preset saves the rests with the subdivision.

## How the meter label reads

The settings row above the dial shows your subdivision and meter. For example:

- **♩ Quarter Notes · 4/4:** quarter notes, no subdivision.
- **♪ Eighth Notes · 7/8 · 2+2+3:** 7/8 in groups of 2+2+3.
- **♩. Dotted-quarter pulse · 6/8 · 3+3:** 6/8 counted in dotted quarters.
- **♬ Septuplets ●●○●●○● · 4/4:** septuplets with rests on notes 3 and 6.

With a subdivision, the first part names the notes you hear, for example **Sixteenth Notes** for 16ths in 4/4. The groups are not shown for **Downbeat**.

<Platform only="android">
On Android the meter comes first and the notes second, for example **7/8 · 2+2+3 ♪ Eighth Notes**. The label sits above the dial, and while the metronome plays, the bar and beat readout takes its place.
</Platform>
